<objective>
Turn the two tables into the numbers Phase 12 submits. `ProgressRepository.recordResult` gains a
`SolveTelemetry` parameter and writes the `level_result` upsert and the `play_session` row inside
the ONE existing `withTransaction` (D-08), under the unchanged `StorageFailSafe` posture. Three
`@Query` projections then produce a fully-populated `ProgressAggregate` — the Challenge ladder
(`MAX(CAST(levelId AS INTEGER))`), the cumulative Challenge score (`SUM(bestScore)` as `Long?`,
D-07), the cross-mode star total, the solved and three-star counts, the per-Classic-board cleared
and perfect sets, and the four `play_session` `EXISTS` predicates — plus the per-board best scores
the five Classic leaderboards need (PGSL-03). `ResultsViewModel` passes its already-coerced
`solveTelemetry` through.

Purpose: the ROADMAP Phase 12 note requires every aggregate to be SQL-backed and never an
in-memory fold, because the Challenge ladder reaches thousands of rows. Plan 12-02 defined the
shape of `ProgressAggregate`; this plan is the only thing that can fill it.
Output: two aggregate queries on `LevelResultDao`, one on `PlaySessionDao`, three projection row
types, a `ClassicLevelIds` ↔ `ClassicBoard` map at the data boundary, `recordResult` writing both
tables atomically, `aggregate()` / `classicBests()` on the repository, and a new
`ProgressAggregateTest` covering every field.

**Boundary — explicitly NOT this plan:** do not create `ProgressSubmitter`, do not touch
`GamesService` / `NoOpGamesService` / `PlayGamesService`, do not add the submitter call inside
`recordSolvedPlayOnce()`, do not call `AchievementRules.evaluate` anywhere, do not name a
leaderboard or achievement ID, and do not add the dirty flag. All of that is plan 12-07, which
consumes the two repository methods this plan exposes. Do not touch the schema either: `2.json`,
`DB_VERSION` and `MIGRATION_1_2` are plan 12-01's and are already correct — this plan adds
`@Query` methods only, which change no table.
