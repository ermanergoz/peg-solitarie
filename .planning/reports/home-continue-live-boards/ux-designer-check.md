## Judgement: will a 56dp thumbnail make the player's moves visible?
Partly, and the spec is honest about which part. At 48dp of board the 7-wide boards get 6.9dp cells: pegs ≈4.7dp (≈12px on the Pixel_9a), holes ≈2.2dp (≈6px). The pattern of pegs reads as density, so a fresh board (32 pegs), a half-cleared one (≈14) and a near-win (≤ 5) are told apart at a glance in both themes, and the chip stops showing the start board the founder complained about. Two limits. (1) Early in a game the difference is two or three 2dp holes among thirty pegs: after one to three moves the thumbnail looks like the start board, which is the moment a player most often leaves a game and a plausible moment for the founder to test; the spoken "N pegs left" carries it for TalkBack users only. (2) The 9-wide boards (5.3dp cells, pegs ≈3.6dp, holes ≈1.7dp) are at the edge; at 1.7dp the dimple's two tones collapse to a smudge, so the holes — which are what "the moves made" are — barely register. Refusing 64dp is right: the chip's height is 15k's and D-03n's input. Within 56dp the picture is the best available without touching `StaticBoardArt`, which CV-AC06 keeps. Accept the pattern-level read as meeting the founder's words; let F-4's device read say whether it is enough; and if the founder repeats the complaint on an early-game save, the next lever is the thumbnail's own hole drawing (a flat dimple with a legibility floor), not the chip's size.
