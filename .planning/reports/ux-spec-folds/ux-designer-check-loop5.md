## Findings

**F-38 — MINOR — D-TBa item 1's order is produced by Compose's geometry only where the board is width-limited; on a height-limited board the full-width banner is read before the slab's group, and the ruling forbids the pin.** `docs/ux-spec.md:1289-1295`, `:4474-4478`, `:4605`, `:5586-5601`
- D-TBa rules: "The slab is one traversal group, so every cell is read before anything that overlaps the slab, including a banner or notice over the lowest rows … The banner and the notice keep D-04g's geometric place and gain no traversal index."
- The layout (`GameScreen.kt:193-252`): the board region `Box` is padded by `board_screen_margin`; the slab is an M3 `Surface` with `widthIn(max = board_max_size).fillMaxWidth().aspectRatio(1)`, centred; `NoWinBanner` is `align(BottomCenter).fillMaxWidth()`, so it spans the region (the hint spec says so too: "the banner spans the board region", `PegSolitarie-hint/docs/ux-spec.md:2916`). Both are M3 `Surface`s and so traversal groups.
- Compose orders the Box's children by rows of vertically overlapping nodes, and inside a row by left edge, then top. A raised banner overlaps the slab's lowest rows, so the two share a row:
  - on the two windows 26b is sat on (411×923 and 360×640 in portrait) the slab is width-limited, its left edge equals the banner's, and the slab's higher top puts the group first: the ruled order, by a tie-break;
  - wherever the board is height-limited — every phone in landscape (orientation is not locked, sign-off O-5), a short window such as `w360dp-h560dp` (region content 296dp against a 328dp slab), and any window wider than 512dp, where the slab caps at 480dp and is centred — the slab's left edge is inside the banner's, and the banner is read first: timer → banner → board stop → cells → dock.
- So the sentence is true on the QA windows and false as a general claim, and loop 4's F-27 asked for exactly this: "the order must be pinned (a traversal group), whichever order is ruled". The group pins the cells among themselves; it does not pin the group against the banner.
- **Owed by the author (one line):** pin the order — a traversal index on the board region's children (the slab before the banner or notice), amending "gain no traversal index" — or cap the banner's width to the slab's, which changes one of §4.4.2's "never colour alone" channels on wide windows only. The pin keeps D-04g's look and reads the same in portrait; it is the recommendation. The brief is being amended now and builds whichever the spec says.

**F-39 — MINOR — "Only while TalkBack is running" names a product, not a condition a test or a device pass can set; the brief's Amendment 1 reads it as touch exploration, which is the faithful and narrowest reading, and the two classes that reading excludes are recorded in the brief, not in the spec.** `docs/ux-spec.md:1338-1340`, `:4619-4626`, `:4689-4691`, `:5725-5734`, `:5774-5775`, `:4903-4904`
- Android has no "TalkBack running" signal. The platform's screen-reader signal is `AccessibilityManager.isTouchExplorationEnabled`, which TalkBack always sets; that is what the brief's Amendment 1 builds (its Open issues, "The touch-exploration gate's two unknowns").
- **The reading is faithful to D-TBa's wording and to W5.** The gate reaches only input focus: the four focus moves (M7) and keyboard Tab. Every stop, description, click action and jump action (M1–M5) stays for every service. The game-designer's W5 defers Switch Access, keyboard and D-pad "as their own judged paths" and asks only that "M4 and M5 should serve them", which the gate leaves whole; M7 is not in that sentence. A screen reader with touch exploration off is the same case: it keeps the board and loses the four moves. The alternatives are worse: a gate on any enabled accessibility service brings the brief's consequence (a) back for a sighted keyboard player with, say, a password-manager service on; a gate on spoken-feedback services (`FEEDBACK_SPOKEN`) covers a reader with touch exploration off but still not Switch Access, and cannot be set in a JVM test as simply.
- **What the text must change.** (1) Name the condition in D-TBa item 5, §11.1 and TB-11: "while touch exploration is enabled (`AccessibilityManager.isTouchExplorationEnabled`), the platform's screen-reader signal, which TalkBack always sets" — so TB-11's "no cell or board stop can take input focus" has a seam a Robolectric test can set, and TB-7's keyboard check names the state it runs in. (2) Record, under the W5 reference at `:4442`, the two excluded classes — a screen reader with touch exploration off, and Switch Access — with what they keep (every stop and action) and what they lose (the four focus moves), so the device pass does not fail them against D-TBa's words. (3) Say that the four moves themselves are attempted only under the same gate: a `FocusRequester` on a node that is not focusable moves nothing, and one whose modifier is absent throws on `requestFocus()`; today the spec gates the modifier and is silent on the call.
- No broader ruling is owed: narrowing to touch exploration is the right trade, and the W5 record makes it a deferral rather than a gap.
