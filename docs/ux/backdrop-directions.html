What every direction keeps

  • The board. Same pegs, holes, selection ring and move dots, on the same solid slab. The board never sits on glass, so it reads exactly as today.
  • The layout. Every card, button, text size and position stays where phase 15 put it. Only the background and the finish of the cards change.
  • The colours. Violet leads and pink seconds. Gold stays reserved for stars and hints.
  • Nothing from Candy Crush. No sweets, no characters, no candy shapes, no tile grid. Only light, glass, gloss and bubbles, drawn by us.
  • Start-up speed. Everything is drawn in code, with no pictures to load. The first frame is a plain gradient, so the app opens as fast as today.
  • Calm titles. The top of every screen stays as quiet as today, so the title and the timer always read clearly.

A · Game · dark

Why this one

The most direct answer to "glassy, with something in the back". Three big, soft pools of violet and pink light drift slowly behind the screen, and every panel you touch becomes frosted glass that lets their colour through. A soft glint crosses the glass every few seconds, so the menu feels alive even when nobody touches it.

The cards keep their violet, pink and gold tints, so the Home you approved in phase 15 stays recognisable. The game screen gets the same light, lower and slower, around a board that stays solid.

What it costs to build in Compose

One Canvas behind each screen draws a gradient and three soft radial circles. One infinite animation moves them in the draw phase only, so nothing recomposes. That is the work Home already does for its two blobs, plus one circle.

The glass is a translucent fill, a 1dp light rim and the existing clay shadow, with no blur pass. Compose has no built-in background blur, RenderEffect needs Android 12 while the app supports Android 7, and over light that is already soft a blur would change almost nothing. This mock uses no blur either, so it shows what we would ship. The glint is one moving gradient per panel. The first frame is the gradient with the pools at rest; motion starts after it. No images, no new library.

Readable text, readable board

No text sits on the moving light. The title and the game title stay in a calm band at the top that always matches today's background. Text on glass reads at least 5.3:1 in light and 6.0:1 in dark even over the brightest overlap of light (body text needs 4.5:1), because the glass is dense: white at 80%, or the dark background at 72%.

The slab stays solid, so pegs and holes are today's, pixel for pixel. Its edge gains a 1dp rim. In light the pools make it stand out more than today (1.9:1 against 1.05:1); in dark the rim or the fill keeps it at 1.28:1 or more (today 1.26:1).

What moves: three light pools on 21 to 29 second loops (two pools, 33 to 39 seconds, on the game screen); a glint crosses each panel every 9 seconds. With animations off, nothing moves.

Candy Crush check (my pre-check; reviewers give the binding answer): no element traces to a King design, and one screenshot reads as a peg-solitaire cross on a lavender slab, not as Candy Crush.

B · Game · dark

Why this one

The richest and most alive of the three, and the closest to the energy you described. A deep violet space sits behind the screen, with two slow, glossy waves of colour near the bottom and soft out-of-focus lights rising through it. Panels get a wet gloss highlight, and the two mode cards become gel: solid violet and pink with white text and a shine across the top.

It is also the biggest change. The mode cards you chose to keep in Q-004 go from pale tints to deep gel, the light theme turns noticeably pink, and the app starts to look like a darker, bolder game.

What it costs to build in Compose

One Canvas draws a gradient, two wave Paths built once per screen size, and nine soft lights (radial gradients with a brighter rim), all on one animation clock: about a dozen fills per frame, still cheap.

The gloss is a gradient overlay and a highlight line per panel. Compose has no inner shadow, so the depth is drawn with gradients instead of one shadow call. The gel cards are a vertical gradient plus one clipped highlight. The first frame is the gradient alone; the waves and lights arrive on the second. No images, no new library.

Readable text, readable board

Panels stay solid under their gloss, and their text sits where the gloss has faded out, so the stats and the Continue card read as today. White text on the gel cards reads at least 5.0:1 on violet and 4.7:1 on pink where the text sits; the shine ends above the title.

In dark, the lights above mid-screen stay dim enough that a title wrapped at the largest font size still reads over them. The slab stays solid with a 1dp rim, which keeps its edge at 1.28:1 or more against the deep background (today 1.26:1).

What moves: two waves bob on 9 and 12 second cycles; nine lights rise and fade on 22 to 40 second loops (five, slower, on the game screen); a shine crosses each mode card every 10 seconds. With animations off, nothing moves.

Candy Crush check (my pre-check): no element traces to a King design. Deep violet with glossy light is a common casual-game finish, and the peg cross on a solid slab rules out a match-3 reading. Soft "bokeh" lights are not on the research-analyst's list word for word, so I ask for a one-line confirmation before anything is built.

C · Game · dark

Why this one

The lightest touch. The sky stays pale and close to today's, two wide ribbons of lilac and pink light sway slowly across it, and a few soap bubbles with shimmering rims drift up and fade. Panels become white glass with soft pink and violet shadows, and the cards keep their exact colours.

It adds life without changing the app's character and gives the board the calmest frame of the three. It is also the least glassy, and the furthest from the energy you described.

What it costs to build in Compose

One Canvas draws a gradient, two rotated soft ellipses and seven bubbles (each a ring drawn with a gradient brush plus a highlight dot) on one animation clock: about 17 fills per frame, all of them small.

Panels are a translucent white fill, a rim and two tinted shadows. The first frame is the gradient alone. No images, no new library.

Readable text, readable board

The pale sky keeps every panel at least as readable as today: white glass at 86% keeps labels at 5.8:1 or more in light, and the dark glass keeps them at 5.4:1 or more. Bubbles fade out before they reach the calm band, and in dark they are faint enough that a wrapped title still reads over them.

The slab stays solid. With its rim, its edge is never weaker than today's in either theme.

What moves: two light ribbons sway on 17 and 21 second cycles; seven bubbles rise and fade on 19 to 30 second loops (one ribbon and four bubbles, slower, on the game screen). With animations off, nothing moves.

Candy Crush check (my pre-check): no element traces to a King design; soap bubbles and light ribbons are generic. Like B's lights, bubbles are not on the research-analyst's list word for word, so they get the same one-line confirmation before build.

Pick

My recommendation

A · Frosted glass

It is the closest match to what you asked for: glass you can see, with soft light moving behind it. It brings real motion to both screens while the cards and colours you chose to keep in Q-004 stay recognisable, and it is the cheapest of the three to build and to run, at about the work Home already does today. The board stays the calmest thing on the screen.

Choose B if you want the boldest, most energetic look and are happy for the app to turn darker and pinker, with gel mode cards. Choose C if you want the smallest step from today.

Whichever you pick replaces the sweets landscape from Q-020: no hills, sweets or sprinkles. Nothing is built until you choose.
